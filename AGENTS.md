# project workflow

- `shared/routes.json` is the canonical route and physics data. run `python3 shared/sync.py` after editing it, before either engine's checks or builds.
- launch the godot prototype with `godot --path godot`; verify with `godot --headless --path godot --script res://check.gd`.
- the unity project is `unity/` and uses unity 6000.3.27f1, input system 1.20.1, and urp 17.3.0. open `unity/Assets/main.unity` and press play, or run the packaged `builds/rougarou-unity.app`.
- run unity's editor checks with `unity -batchmode -nographics -quit -projectPath unity -executeMethod Check.Run -logFile unity-check.log`, substituting the installed editor executable if `unity` is not on the path. rebuild the mac app by changing `Check.Run` to `Check.Build`.
- the shared simulation runs at 60 hz. the first bakeoff climber is a physical torso with four spring contacts and procedural visual limbs, not a full articulated character. use `shared/results.json` for the validated decision and known gaps.
- the unreal project is `unreal/` (engine 5.8, c++ module `Rougarou`). it is now the main build and the source of truth for climbing rules. the model lives in `unreal/Source/Rougarou/ClimbSim.*`; unity (`unity/Assets/Main.cs`) is the original spec and lacks the later mechanics: free reaching with shoulder reach, core tension, reach-driven hips, forearm pump, and match-the-top finishes.
- verify unreal headlessly with `"/Users/Shared/Epic Games/UE_5.8/Engine/Binaries/Mac/UnrealEditor.app/Contents/MacOS/UnrealEditor" unreal/Rougarou.uproject -ExecCmds="Automation RunTests Rougarou; Quit" -unattended -nullrhi -log`. see `unreal/PLAN.md` for the roadmap.
- in unreal, `shared/routes.json` only supplies physics constants; the boulder problems are set on the gym's own panels in `unreal/Source/Rougarou/GymActor.cpp` (`WallSections`, `Problems`). problems run up stacks of flat angled panels (`FClimbPanel`), and `Rougarou.ClimbSim` must prove every one can be sent. launch with `-route=N` to start on a problem and `-shot` to save `unreal/Saved/gym.png` and `shot.png` and quit.
- `web/` is a no-build three.js port of the unreal sim, gym and climber (`sim.js` mirrors `ClimbSim.cpp`, `gym.js` mirrors `GymActor.cpp` and `HoldShapes.h`, `climber.js` mirrors `ClimberActor.cpp`). change both together. `node web/check.mjs` is the web solvability check; pushing to `main` deploys `web/` to https://beerooyay.github.io/rougarou/ after that check passes. serve it locally with `python3 -m http.server 8765 --directory web`.
- bare wall (`Smeared`) is friction only: it never takes a pull, and grips with `Friction` times `Squeeze`, the force pressing the body into the wall (slab lean plus hands on holds, scaled by each hold's incut). limb `Masses` add torque about the hips, which is what makes flags and barn doors work. a fall sets `Fallen` and stops the sim; unreal restarts on it, the web game hands the pose to `web/ragdoll.js`.
- the web build's look runs ahead of unreal's: smooth holds with bolt washers, the t-nut hole grid (`holes` from `buildGym`), birch and charcoal panels, and the climber's boxy tee (a spring-hung hem), shorts, buzz cut and beard. port these when the unreal climber gets the same pass.
- web ui: `ui.js` (menu, settings, tick list in local storage), `recorder.js` (ported from rawah, clips in indexeddb), `audio.js` (synthesized sounds feeding the recorder), `editor.js` (route setter; sets saved per problem name in local storage, holds carry a stable `seed`). limbs, fingers and shoes are swept tubes (`tube.js`). `Restart` now centers the hips on the four start holds, in both builds.
- the web game is also a walkable gym (`web/world.js` collision, `walkPose` in `web/climber.js`): problems start from their two start holds and `Route.grade` labels each one. `window.rougarou.tick(seconds)` steps the game without the browser's frame clock, for testing in a hidden pane.
- climbing feel lives in `FClimbSim::Step`: limbs travel to `Tips` at human speed and only bear weight once `Latch` is set on arrival (lower when the body is moving, the deadpoint rule), the hips ease toward `Goal`, and slipping builds up in `Slips` from `Strain` over 1 instead of a fixed timer.
- first person climbing (`fp` in `web/main.js`, V toggles, `window.rougarou.fp`): the camera blends from the third-person shot to the eye (`pose.head` pulled back 4 cm and kept at least 30 cm off the wall by `awayFromWall` in `climber.js`, near plane 0.03, vertical fov 80). yaw and pitch are in the wall's own frame (smoothed panel normal and up, so a panel kink does not snap the view). the head parts swap to a colorWrite-off material (`Climber.setFirstPerson`) and the climber stops casting shadows so its own shadow does not cover the wall; the tee collar is closed by a cap. `holdInSight` picks the hold nearest the line of sight (scaled by distance) and a glow sprite rings it; `limbKey` takes r/l then a/l within 1.6 s (r a right hand, l a left hand, r l right foot, l l left foot) and `sendLimb` calls `sim.grab`, so reach and hip-open rules apply as before. elbows and knees are pushed out of the wall in `climbPose`. R is not retry in this view. screenshots in the preview pane lag a couple of seconds behind the canvas: copy the canvas to a 2d overlay in the same task as `tick()` before screenshotting. in first person the hips shift with the arrow keys, latched (`fp.lean`, space clears), and `climber.away` (eased with `fp.k`) hangs the hips 22 cm and chest 18 cm further off the wall and splays the knees, with the eye 12 cm forward of the head and at least 18 cm off the wall, so looking down shows the legs.
- joint limits in `solve()` (`climber.js`): an elbow or knee bends only toward its pole side, the pole is steadied by `pose.back` so it cannot flip when the limb lines up with it, it never hyperextends (a limb asked past its length stretches), and it folds no tighter than `Folds` (legs 30 degrees, arms 35, the angle between the two bones): a hand or foot asked closer than that slides out to the tightest fold and carries its palm or foot with it (`slide`; at the start of the nine problems the biggest shift is 2 cm). not done: swing and twist limits at the shoulder, hip, neck and spine.
- body joint limits (`limitJoints` in `climber.js`, run first in `solve()`, table `Joints`, audit `limitStats`): in the torso's own frame (front away from `pose.back`, up the spine, outward from the body's middle) a shoulder may point 50 degrees behind its line and cross the body freely, a hip 60 behind, 85 out and 70 across; the spine twists 50 degrees between hips and shoulders (the shoulders turn back to the limit) and the neck turns 80 each way, 55 down and 65 up on `pose.look`. a hand or foot asked past a swing limit is carried back to the edge with its palm or shoe (`slide`). the first values (hip 35 behind, 70 out, 45 across) moved feet up to 13 cm off their holds in random play, because the sim's torso leans in on steep walls and hips hang out; the current ones moved the worst foot 6 cm in one rare case and most routes 0-2. the first-person view turns 137 degrees each way (`fpLook`). not done: independent spine bend (the torso is one segment between the roots), wrist and ankle limits, and ragdoll poses are not limited.
- the climber is a skinned character (`web/figure.js`, `Figure`, worn by `Climber.wear`; `?tubes` on the URL keeps the old tubes, and so does a failed load). the model is `web/assets/character/climber.glb` (2 MB, 101 bones, body, boxers, eyes, teeth, 1024 px webp textures), made from a Character Creator 4 FBX (CC3_Base_Plus rig, 193 MB with 300 blend shapes) by `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --python tools/convert_character.py -- <character.fbx> <out.glb>`; the script drops the shapes, the camera, the dummy boxes and the second armature and folds each separate opacity map into its diffuse's alpha. `Figure.apply(pose)` turns the pose the sim and `walkPose` already make into bones: the pelvis lands on the pose's hips, the spine and neck blend from the pelvis frame to the chest frame to the head's look, each arm and leg is two-bone IK from the bone's own shoulder or hip to the pose's wrist or ankle (the pole is the pose's elbow or knee, and the upper bone is rolled so the joint bends the way it does at rest, forward for an elbow and back for a knee), the hand and foot take the pose's frames (`hand.over/thumb/out`, `foot.toe/heel/dorsum`, so `walkPose` and `climbPose` both give feet a `dorsum`), the ankle is placed so the toe tip lands on the pose's toe, and the fingers curl by grip (`Curl`). rests are read from the file (rest world rotation and position per bone), so nothing assumes the rig's bone axes; everything runs in three's right-handed meters after `toThree`. the left and right hands are checked against the pose's back-of-hand (`figure.checks`, 0 mismatches). in first person (`firstPerson`) the head skin, lashes, eyes and teeth are hidden and the camera sits 9 cm ahead of the eye bones. `Climber.setOutfit` tints the boxers with the shorts color; shirt and shoe colors do nothing now. not done: no hair, shirt or shoes (the export is a bald body in boxers), the ragdoll fall looks crumpled, no clavicle or shoulder-blade motion, toes and the thumb stay at rest, the eyes and jaw do not move, and looking straight down in first person shows the chest, not the legs. the dev server must send no-store (browsers reuse the modules otherwise): `python3 -m http.server` is not enough in the Browser pane.
- the AI climber (`web/agent.js` body, `web/brain.js` head, `tools/devserver.py` server; Settings -> ai climber, B watches it): a second `Climber` (own `Figure`, own `Sim` so it and the player can share a wall) that tours the gym once (`memory.toured`), then works through the problems by grade, easiest first (`order()`, one send each: `NEED`), V1 being `pour` and `arch leg`. a go is: walk to the start spot, `begin()` (a blend into `climbPose`), then repeated decisions: `decide()` rehearses every (hip shift, limb, hold) on `Sim.clone()` copies (`rehearse`: 0.75 s each, safe if both hands stay on), asks the model to pick one (`Brain.ask` -> POST /ai/chat, JSON answer `{think, candidate, custom, note}`, `choose` maps it to a legal move; a bad answer falls back on the best safe rehearsal), then a job runs the shift for 0.5 s and the grab. a send, a fall (ragdoll, then up and back to the start), a give up (40 moves or a minute without a higher hand) each write one line to `Memory` (`attempts`, `sent`, and the model's own one-line `notes` per problem), which goes into the next prompt (`describe`) and is saved by the server in `tools/.cache/ai-memory.json` (git-ignored). hard mode (`aimode: blind`, `blindList`) skips the rehearsal: the model is only told what it can reach and must learn from falls. the server keeps the key in its environment only (`OPENAI_API_KEY`, optional `OPENAI_MODEL` default gpt-4o-mini), answers only to this machine, and stops after `AI_MAX_CALLS` (400) calls a run. measured: with rehearsal and no key the climber sent `pour` in 13 moves (21 s) and went on to `arch leg`; the OpenAI path itself was only tested with a stand-in model (no key was available), so the prompt in `brain.js` is untuned. not done: no tuning of the prompt against real answers, the tour is not skipped when memory says it was done on another machine, the AI does not shout when it sends, it never walks away to rest, and it is not shared over the network (it is each browser's own).
- the AI's instructions are `web/instructions.js`: `SYSTEM` (about 1,500 words: how the sim works in numbers, how a good climber moves, never loop, how to learn from notes and human demos, the two modes, the JSON answer) and `TEACHER` (what to take from watching a human). keep its numbers in step with `sim.js`. loops are stopped three ways: the prompt lists RECENT MOVES and a STUCK line after 3 moves without a higher hand or hips; a model move that repeats one of the last 8 is swapped for a fresh safe candidate once it is stuck (`decide`); and after `FLAT` (10) moves with no progress, or `MOVES` (30), or `STALL` (40 s), it jumps down and tries again, writing the reason to its notebook. a model that takes over 14 s is dropped (`Brain.ask` abort) and its best rehearsal used. "watches me" (`aimode: watch`, `apprentice`): the AI stands beside the player's problem (`mode_shadow`) or follows them; main wraps the player's `sim.grab` and calls `agent.watchStart/watchMove/watchEnd` (begin, each hold, send, fall, jump down, retry) and every climb of 2+ moves goes into `memory.data.demos` (latest 3 sends and 2 falls per problem); a model call with `TEACHER` turns it into up to two notes, and when the AI climbs that problem its prompt lists the demos and a starred candidate marks the human's next step it has not matched (`demoNext`), which its fallback also prefers. tested with a stand-in model (a repeat-the-first-candidate stub: it gave up after 10 flat moves rather than looping); not yet with OpenAI.
- the AI as route setter (`aimode: set`, `web/setter.js`, `web/ladder.js`, `web/grader.js`; his name is Tuck, persona in `instructions.js` `PERSONA`, the brief in `SETTER`; he speaks short lines over his head, `Agent.say`): `Setter.cycle` is an async loop: `choose` a route nobody is climbing (the one set longest ago), `design` it (model JSON `{name, grade, quip, holds}` through `Brain.ask` with `SETTER`, parsed and cleaned by `parse`; or `generate`, his own seeded generator, up to 14 seeds), `check` it (counts and bounds, 18 cm minimum spacing, at least a third of the holds new, `startsHold` (a body can hang on the starts), then `grader.solve`: the AI's own rehearsal lookahead climbs a copy of the problem from the start holds and must send it within 32 moves, with the reason, the height it stopped at and the nearest holds as the rejection text; then the grade), up to 3 rounds with the reasons fed back, then `build`: `plan` turns the diff into edits by hold reference (shift, add, remove; far shifts become take then put) ordered floor jobs first and ladder jobs left to right; each `work` walks to `access(route, spot)` (the floor in front of the wall if the spot is under 2.05 m, otherwise a ladder: top on the wall 1.35 m over the feet, base found from the 75 degree lean, rung coordinate `q`), raises the ladder (`raisePose`), climbs it a rung at a time (`ladderPose`: right hand, left foot, left hand, right foot, each limb stepping over `STEP` of a rung with the other three on, hips kept in front of the ladder at their own height and the torso tipped toward it), works the hold with one hand and a rail in the other (`workPose`), changes the route's data at the middle of the job (`apply`, then `hooks.rebuild`, a chalk puff; a carried hold is flagged `carried`, which `routeHolds` skips, and drawn as a blob in the hand), climbs down and stows or keeps the ladder if the next job is within 70 cm. then `test`: the agent climbs the real problem up to three goes (`Agent.testing`, called from `record`); if it cannot send, the solver's stopping place goes to a second design round and a second test, and if that fails the old holds are put back by the same build (`revert`). the problem is renamed and regraded (`routes[i].name/grade`), stored by `Editor.commitRoute` under the original name (holds in `rougarou-sets`, name and grade in `rougarou-setter`), the sign repainted (`retag`) and his memory of the old layout cleared (`Memory.reset`). the grade is a straight line fitted to the nine problems by least squares (`gradeOf`: -0.45 + 3.16 hold cost + 5.24 steepness, rms 0.7 of a grade; the label stays the asked grade if the measure is within 0.6 of it). measured: the solver takes about 0.1 s a design and sends all nine original problems; his generator passes the checks 20 to 90 percent of the time depending on grade and wall; a whole cycle with no key ran design, ladder work and the test climb without errors (about 4 minutes of game time for 17 edits). not done: the ladder does not avoid other things on the floor, he does not move the ladder sideways (he stows it and stands it again), a hold in his hand is a blob not the real mesh, the shirt, shoes and hair are not on the model, and the model's own designs were only seen once (it copied the old holds then, which the 'too much like the current set' rule now rejects).
- setter pace and clipping: tasks run at `TEMPO` 1.25 times real time with a ladder at `RUNGS` 1.4 rungs a second (down 1.3 times faster), a hold on or off in 2.2 s, the ladder raised in 1.6 s and put away in 1.2 s, and he stays up the ladder between jobs from the same spot and walks it sideways (`shuffle`) for jobs under 1.3 m away; 20 edits took about 200 s of game time (floor jobs about 3 s, ladder jobs 8 to 12 s). holds may not touch: `Setter.clashes` rejects any pair of holds closer than half their widths added (`SIZE` in setter.js as gym.js draws them, a 5 percent margin and 2 cm), any hold on a scattered hold (`gym.decorHolds`, recorded by `buildGym` without changing its dice order; about 100 are near each wall) and any hold straddling a fold between panels, and `Setter.repair` pushes overlapping holds apart, off the scattered ones and off the folds before every check, so most designs pass (clashes 28 -> 1, 10 -> 0, 30 -> 0, 25 -> 0, 24 -> 2, 26 -> 0 on six walls, and the check still rejects what is left). the model's holds go through the same repair and check, with the clash in words as the reason if it fails.
- The Wall (`web/wallgen.js` pure data and the solver check, `web/thewall.js` the meshes, the door and the sign, `tools/wall_check.mjs` the node check, `web/wall-hints.json`): a door in the front wall of the gym (`Door` in gym.js: x 2560, y 0, 130 cm wide, the front wall is three blocks around the gap and `World.solid` keeps the gap shut until `world.doorOpen`), a sign above it, E opens it from either side (`DoorWay`), and a room behind it (`Room`: x 2570 to 4400, 18 m wide, side walls a kilometer tall, mats, no ceiling, fog only inside it) with the wall at the end, facing -x. The Wall is the last entry of `routes` (an `endless` `Wall extends Route`: its `holds` array only grows so hold indices stay stable, `at(y)` is O(1), `near(p)` gives the few panels around a point and `climber.awayFromWall`, the ragdoll and `cursor()` use it; `Sim` never sends it; the problem list shows it with the best height from `localStorage rougarou-wall`; the AI, the setter, the editor and `visit()` skip it). Everything is a function of the chunk number: panels are 2.5 m, a chunk is four panels (10 m), the first panel of every chunk is gentle and carries a fixed rest band (`band(y)`: feet jugs 1.25 m under hand jugs at +-0.35, three foot chips), the other three follow a theme chosen once per chunk from a hash (vertical, slab, lean, steep, roof; roofs only above 30 m; more steep and roof with height; `difficulty(h)` = 1 - exp(-h/160) sets the gap between holds 0.34 to 0.70 m, how many are jugs, how far across the wall they spread), the lean cannot turn more than 22 degrees from one panel to the next, and the wall's drift into the room is kept between about -6.5 m and +1.5 m by choosing slabs when it has leaned too far. the wall is 6.4 m wide and has no single path: four LINES (`LINES`: x -1.6, -0.55, 0.55, 1.6, nothing past 2.5 m from the middle; the ones on the climber's left are harder than the height alone says and the ones on the right easier) each in its own color (`Colors`: green, blue, orange, red from easy to hard, white cross holds, yellow rest bands; each hold has a `line` number and `build` makes one mesh per color) each run from a chunk's band to the next one, wandering out toward their own place on the wall and back, and `crosses` puts holds between neighbouring lines (level with the holds of one, about 0.65 m from both) so a climber can change line anywhere. each line comes from `middle(k, attempt, wall, line)` (attempts 0-9 random, 10 and 11 kinder, 12 an all-jug ladder), made not to touch each other, the band, the lines before it or a fold in the wall (`repair`), and `makeChunk` climbs a mini-route of the chunk (its band, that line, the next band's hands as the finish: both hands on them) with `grader.solve` and takes the first attempt that is sent, line by line; the two middle lines (`CORE`) are flattened and made a ladder if they must, an outer line that will not go is left out of that chunk, then the whole chunk with its cross holds is climbed once more (if that fails only the lines are kept). about 200 holds a chunk and 2 s of solving; the meshes are well rounded only within a chunk of the climber (`build(k, fine)`). measured with `node tools/wall_check.mjs 300`: all 300 chunks (3 km) are climbable, half on the first or second try, the rest on the kinder ones, about 2 s a chunk, and the same digest on every run. the integer hash and the seeded generator involve no floating point until the end, but the solver's choices could in principle differ between engines, so `wall-hints.json` (`--write`) stores the attempt that worked for each line of each of the first 300 chunks and `makeChunk` tries that one first (and still checks it). the game grows it three chunks ahead of the climber (`ensure`, async, one chunk at a time), draws the chunks from three below to five above (`show`), and follows the climber with a point light. height is hip height above the mats (`heightOf`). a fall or jump from above 6 m starts `startLower`: an auto-belay lowers you at 5 m/s to the mats (2.5 to 8 s), then `getUp`. not done: no way to share best heights between players, the wall has no top so it is never sent, nothing is saved but the best height, and the AI does not climb it.

- chalk (`web/chalkbag.js`, `web/chalking.js`, chalk in `sim.js`, the bag and hands in `figure.js`, the reach in `climber.js`): `Sim.chalk[limb]` (1 fresh, 0 bare; hands only) wears off while a hand hangs on a hold, `0.006 + 0.03 * strain + 0.02 * pump` a second, and a bare hand holds `0.8 + 0.2 * chalk` of what a chalked one does; - form lab (`web/formlab.js`, `web/wallpose.js`, F6 / Option + J / Settings -> tools, `rougarou.formLab`): trains on the wall itself and the joints obey anatomy. `Anatomy` in `limits.js` is the hard-wall table (adult ROM from AAOS/Norkin and White, Kapandji, Boone and Azen, Soucie, White and Panjabi;
